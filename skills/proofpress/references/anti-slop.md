# Anti-slop passes

The aim isn't to "hide AI". It's to remove the habits that make text feel generic, padded or salesy, so a busy reader actually gets to the end. The scanner (`slop_scan` / `proofpress scan`) catches the mechanical things; the reading passes catch the rest. The word lists below are English; for other languages, apply the same ideas (hype, corporate filler, fake hooks, empty transitions, padding) by reading.

## Pass 1: obvious patterns

### Hype words (cut, or replace with something specific)
game-changer, revolutionary, cutting-edge, seamless(ly), effortless(ly), robust, powerful, ultimate, comprehensive, unparalleled, next-level, supercharge, unlock (as a metaphor), elevate, empower, transform(ative), skyrocket, must-have, top-notch, world-class, best-in-class, state-of-the-art, hassle-free, magic(al)

### Corporate verbs
leverage, utilize, facilitate, optimize, streamline, harness, delve, navigate (the landscape), foster, bolster, spearhead, embark, endeavor, enhance

→ use: use, help, make easier, cut, speed up, look at, deal with, start

### Fake hook questions
"Ever wondered...?", "Have you ever...?", "Tired of...?", "Struggling to...?", "What if I told you...?", "Sound familiar?", "Ready to...?"
→ open with the answer instead.

### Empty marketing phrases
"In today's fast-paced world", "In the ever-evolving landscape", "When it comes to", "It's no secret that", "Look no further", "Whether you're X or Y", "Take your X to the next level", "the world of", "a testament to", "plays a crucial/vital/pivotal role", "it's important to note", "it's worth noting", "at the end of the day", "in conclusion", "in summary", "without further ado", "dive in / deep dive", "buckle up", "we've got you covered", "say goodbye to", "rest assured", "peace of mind"

### Unsupported claims
- Superlatives without evidence ("the most accurate", "the #1 app").
- Statistics without a linked source ("80% of people forget...").
- Fake experience ("we tested 15 apps", "as a former X, I...").
- Guarantees ("never miss again", "100% accurate").
→ remove, or replace with a cited fact or an honest hedge.

## Pass 2: subtle signals

- **Repetitive sentence openings.** Three sentences in a paragraph starting "This", "You", "It", or a run of "-ing" openers. Vary structure, not just the first word.
- **Missing contractions** (English). "You do not need to" → "You don't need to". Aim for about one contraction per 60 to 80 words of body prose. Formal citations can stay formal.
- **Weak transitions.** "Additionally,", "Furthermore,", "Moreover,", "In addition,", "Overall,", "Ultimately,", "That said," at the start of many paragraphs. Usually just delete them; paragraph order carries the logic.
- **Long paragraphs.** Anything over about 70 words or 4 sentences: split or cut.
- **Em dashes and en dashes used as dashes.** Not allowed in body text. Use a comma, a full stop, parentheses or a colon. Hyphens in compound words and ranges written with "to" are fine.
- **Rule of three on autopilot.** "Fast, simple, and reliable." Use the number of items that's true.
- **Section-ending summaries** that restate the section. Cut them.
- **Symmetrical bullets.** Every bullet the same length and shape, each starting with a bolded noun and a colon. Mix it up or use prose.
- **Hedging stacks.** "can potentially help you possibly..." Pick one hedge or none.
- **Vague nouns.** "various factors", "a variety of options", "key aspects". Name them.
- **Chatbot residue.** "Great question", "I hope this helps", "Let's explore", "Here's the thing".
- **"Not X, but Y" / "It's not just X, it's Y"** more than once.

## Good voice, briefly

Follow `context/voice.md`. When it's silent: a knowledgeable colleague, direct and practical; specific beats general ("the $30 you tip out to the bar" beats "your tip-out obligations"); short and long sentences mixed; second person; plain words; admit trade-offs, including your own product's.

## Before / after

Before: "In today's fast-paced world, keeping your plants alive is crucial. Whether you're a beginner or an expert, leveraging a robust plant care app can seamlessly streamline your routine."

After: "Most houseplants die from too much water, not too little. A reminder that knows each plant's schedule fixes that; a sticky note on the fridge fixes it too, if you only have three plants."
