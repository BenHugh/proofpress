# Search intent playbook

Decide intent from the live SERP, not from the keyword alone. If the top five results are all downloadable templates, the searcher wants a template, however the keyword is worded.

## The intent types

### 1. Buying guide / "best X"
The searcher is choosing a tool or product and wants a shortlist with honest trade-offs.
- Must deliver: 4 to 8 options with who each is for, platform, price model (from each listing), standout features and real downsides. A quick-pick table near the top. Selection criteria stated openly.
- Your product: include it, disclose it's yours (the disclosure line in `context/product.md`), and don't automatically rank it first for every reader. Give it the category it actually wins.
- Pitfalls: inventing features or prices for competitors; fake "we tested" claims; ranking without criteria.

### 2. Comparison
The searcher is weighing two or more approaches (app vs spreadsheet, product A vs product B).
- Must deliver: a side-by-side table, a clear "choose X if / choose Y if" verdict, and the deciding factor.

### 3. How-to
The searcher wants to do something now.
- Must deliver: numbered steps a tired person can follow, a worked example with numbers, the common mistakes, and what to do with the result.

### 4. Definition / explainer
The searcher wants to understand a term or rule.
- Must deliver: the definition in the first two sentences, then why it matters to this reader, an example, and the edge cases. Regional differences when the topic is legal or financial.

### 5. Troubleshooting
The searcher has a problem.
- Must deliver: likely causes in order of likelihood, the fix for each, and when to escalate (and to whom).

### 6. List / resource (including "list (download)")
The searcher wants a collection, or a file.
- Must deliver: the items themselves, each usable on its own. For template, checklist and spreadsheet keywords, the actual downloadable file comes first, then how to use it, a filled example, and only then a mention that the product removes the manual work. Be honest that the file is a perfectly good option for many people.

## Audience and region focus

For each article decide whether it is:
- **Region-specific** (anything with laws, taxes, forms, official programmes): name the region in the brief and title where it helps.
- **Region-split** (one section per region, never mixing rules in one sentence).
- **Universal** (templates, tool lists): keep law out, or link to the region explainer.

## Information gain checklist

Before outlining, list at least two things this article gives that the current top results don't:
- A worked example with real-looking numbers the reader can copy.
- A free download that actually works.
- A decision table ("if you X, use Y").
- Official-source citations where competitors just assert.
- Honest limits, including your own product's.
- Role- or situation-specific detail the generic results skip.
